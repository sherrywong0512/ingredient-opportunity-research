# Existing-Case Improvement Protocol

Use this protocol when revising an existing ingredient report, increasing its research-quality score, or preparing case studies for external review.

## 1. Preserve the Baseline

Record the report version, cutoff date, prompt, source-access conditions, requested deliverables, hard-gate results, rubric result, and known limitations before editing. Do not silently replace the baseline or compare reports produced under materially different scopes as if the score change came only from the workflow.

## 2. Improve in Gate Order

Apply this order:

1. exact ingredient identity and supplier-product applicability;
2. core target-market regulation and finished-product claim boundaries;
3. property-to-application trace and direct technical evidence;
4. foreseeable adverse effects and failure modes;
5. current SKU/adoption coverage and potential-customer qualification;
6. comparable price, use amount, use cost, and replacement economics;
7. executable validation actions and target-language quality.

If a hard gate caps the result, close that gate before adding narrative detail or optimizing lower-impact rubric dimensions. A longer report is not an improved report unless new evidence changes completeness, confidence, or the next decision.

## 3. Require Evidence-Producing Changes

Classify each proposed revision as:

- **evidence closure:** adds a missing primary regulation, original study, current label, formal quotation, transaction, or other decision-grade evidence;
- **coverage closure:** defines and completes a reproducible company, SKU, literature, price, or jurisdiction universe;
- **decision conversion:** turns a known gap into a controlled experiment, RFQ, label audit, regulatory request, or customer-validation action with a success rule;
- **presentation only:** changes structure, wording, formatting, or explanation without changing evidence.

Presentation-only work may improve usability but must not raise an evidence-based score. Do not award points twice when one new source supports several duplicated claims.

### What counts as closing common gaps

- **Regulation:** record the exact ingredient identity, jurisdiction, legal instrument/version, food category, permitted amount in the source's original unit and wording, population, source/strain or specification restriction, and claim/label caveat. `GMP`, `quantum satis`, and `按生产需要适量使用` are legal-use rules, not formulation recommendations.
- **Family or grade ingredients:** build a member/grade matrix. A bounded official search may close an approval-status gap when it reports the cutoff, searched authority, exact approved members, and members for which no approval notice was found. “No notice found” must not be rewritten as prohibited or permanently unapproved.
- **Adoption:** define the account/SKU/channel/date universe and require a market-specific current complete label or equivalent product record before calling a company a current user. Zero accessible labels can close the coverage-disclosure gap, but it does not prove non-use and does not earn the same credit as verified adoption.
- **Use amount and cost:** keep legal maximum, study formulation amount, supplier recommendation, customer commercial amount, list price, quotation, and transaction price in separate fields. Calculate use cost only when the numerator, denominator, purity basis, currency, tax/freight basis, and date are compatible.
- **Customer priority:** company growth or growth of the relevant business can raise priority only when the growth period, metric, source, and relevance to the ingredient-using category are stated. It cannot replace evidence of a formulation problem, active project, qualified supplier route, or buying window.

## 4. Reassess Without Moving the Goalposts

After revision:

1. rerun every applicable hard gate;
2. rescore all rubric dimensions, including those expected not to change;
3. cite the exact new evidence behind each score increase;
4. retain remaining blockers and downgrade conclusions that still depend on unknown supplier, customer, regulatory, price, or technical inputs;
5. report `before -> after`, the cap removed or retained, evidence added, and decisions newly unlocked.

Map every score increase to one of these outputs: a hard gate closed, a reproducible universe completed, a new decision-grade source added, an uncertainty narrowed, or an executable decision newly enabled. If no such mapping exists, keep the prior score.

Do not change the scoring interpretation, evidence cutoff, or application scope merely to obtain a higher score. If scope changes, treat the result as a new case rather than a clean before/after comparison.

## 5. Portfolio Publication Rules

For every published example, show:

`case version/date | research scope | evidence cutoff | quality status | hard-gate blockers | score and rubric version | public/proprietary source boundary | time-sensitive refresh warning`

Do not publish an unresolved report as decision-ready. A directional case can still be valuable when it demonstrates correct identity handling, conservative conclusions, explicit blockers, and executable next actions.

Before external publication, check for secrets, personal data, local paths, confidential formulas, non-public prices, unauthorized customer information, copyrighted source reproduction, and stale regulatory or product claims. Keep source links and short attributed findings; do not embed inaccessible full-text publications unless distribution is authorized.
