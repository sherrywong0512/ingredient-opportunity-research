# Food Regulatory Audit Protocol

Use this protocol for food-ingredient feasibility reports. Unless the user limits geography, audit China, the United States, and the European Union together in one comparison table. Use current official legal texts or regulator databases; do not rely on supplier summaries when primary sources are available.

## Determine the Legal Route Before Searching Categories

- **China:** Check the current GB 2760, including GB 2760-2024 while effective, then applicable NHC announcements, food-nutrition fortifier standards, new-food-ingredient approvals, product standards, and population-specific rules. Treat the Foodmate Download Center (`https://down.foodmate.net/`) as an accepted, trustworthy retrieval source for Chinese standards and announcement texts; record the exact standard/announcement number, version, amendment or correction status, clause or table, and access date. Prefer the current corrected text when the site records an erratum. If the ingredient is absent from GB 2760 but permitted through another route, state `not listed in GB 2760; governed by [instrument]`. Do not mark it prohibited merely because it is absent from GB 2760.
- **United States:** Determine whether the intended use relies on a food-additive regulation in 21 CFR, an FDA GRAS notice and response, an independent GRAS conclusion, prior sanction, color-additive rule, dietary-supplement route, or another basis. Record the exact identity, specification, notifier or manufacturer relevance, food categories, use level, and conditions. `FDA has no questions` is not the same as FDA approval.
- **European Union:** Determine whether the ingredient is governed by the food-additives rules, Union List of Novel Foods, nutrients rules, flavourings, enzymes, supplements, or another framework. Record the exact Union-list entry or regulation, conditions of use, maximum level or quantum satis, specifications, labelling, population restrictions, and any data-protection period.

For every jurisdiction, distinguish:

- `permitted for the exact use`;
- `permitted subject to a maximum level or quantum satis/GMP`;
- `permitted through another legal route, not the main additive list`;
- `not found—regulatory review required`;
- `not permitted for the proposed category/population`.

Absence from a searched list is not proof of prohibition. Record the databases and legal instruments checked, search terms, version/date, and access date.

For restrictions and warnings, first search the applicable regulation, approval attachment, labelling rule, safety assessment, and relevant human-tolerance or adverse-effect evidence. Report the strongest supported preliminary conclusion, then state only the remaining decision that needs further regulatory or expert review. Do not use `further review required` as a substitute for available research.

Enumerate every authorised category relevant to the stated downstream scope, not only the analyst's preferred application. State the coverage rule. If the legal list is too long for the main report, show the proposed and shortlisted categories in the main table and attach the complete relevant-category extract as an appendix; never imply that a partial extract is exhaustive.

Keep legally and scientifically distinct quantities separate: product use level, notified or assessed use level, GMP or quantum satis, intake guidance, safety or tolerability boundary, exposure estimate, and mandatory warning-trigger level. They answer different questions and must not be substituted for one another.

## Required Joint Table

Write one concise China–United States–European Union table in the main report. Use one row for each jurisdiction-and-category combination; never compress multiple food categories with different limits or conditions into one cell. If the relevant category list is long, show the proposed and shortlisted uses in the main table and attach the complete relevant-category extract as an appendix.

| Country/region | Legal basis and status | Relevant permitted category/intended use | Use level and regulatory basis | Restrictions, warnings, or supplier applicability | Application conclusion, official source, and gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

In `Use level and regulatory basis`, distinguish the applicable legal use-level concept and preserve the regulator's terminology. Put intake guidance, exposure or safety boundaries, tolerability limits, and warning-trigger conditions under `Restrictions, warnings, or supplier applicability`; never present them as the product-use level unless the legal text explicitly does so.

Use the unit and basis exactly as written by the regulator. Do not convert `g/kg`, `g/L`, `mg/serving`, dry-matter limits, ready-to-consume limits, or quantum satis into one number unless the conversion assumptions are explicit.

## Application Gate

For every shortlisted application, cite the applicable row in the joint table. Mark the gate:

- `pass` only when the exact ingredient/product and proposed category, population, dose, and supplier authorization fit;
- `conditional` when a calculation, product classification, supplier-specific reliance, or legal interpretation remains;
- `fail` when the proposed use exceeds a limit or falls outside an authorization;
- `unresolved` when no primary legal basis was verified.

In the property-to-application map, repeat the result for each jurisdiction with the decisive legal route, product category, population boundary, use condition, and source. Do not convert an intake, exposure, safety, tolerability, or warning condition into a formulation maximum unless the legal instrument explicitly defines it that way.
