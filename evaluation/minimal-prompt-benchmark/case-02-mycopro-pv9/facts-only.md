# Synthetic facts only: MycoPro-PV9

- `MycoPro-PV9` is heat-inactivated biomass from fictional fungal strain `Paecilomyces variotii PV9`, produced on a defined sugar substrate. Pilot material is 65% crude protein on a dry basis. Amino-acid digestibility, residual nucleic acids, mycotoxin panel, batch variability and kill-step validation are not provided.
- A 28-day adult-dog study tested biomass from strain `P. variotii KCL-24`, not PV9, at 4% and 8% inclusion in an extruded diet. The pack reports similar apparent total-tract protein digestibility at 4%, lower voluntary intake during days 1–4 at 8%, and no serious adverse event. It did not test cats, growth, reproduction, allergy, long-term use, full protein replacement or PV9.
- An internal PV9 bench test replaced 20% of chicken by-product meal by weight in one dog-kibble formula. It reports acceptable extrusion appearance but contains no control diet, palatability, digestibility, amino-acid adequacy, stool, bloodwork, shelf-life or statistical analysis.
- No PV9 or KCL-24 cat feeding study is available. The pack contains no data on taurine, arginine, sulfur amino acids, arachidonic acid or preformed vitamin A adequacy in a complete cat diet.
- A public GRAS notice covers fictional strain `P. variotii PV7` for specified US human foods. It does not name PV9, animal food or complete pet food. The pack contains no FDA animal-food consultation, AAFCO definition or state registration for PV9.
- A search note says the exact name `PV9 fungal biomass` was not found in a provided incomplete excerpt of China's Feed Materials Catalogue. No exact-product approval or legal opinion is provided.
- A report values the global pet-food market at USD 140 billion. It gives no complete-food output, protein-system share, adoption rate or PV9 inclusion rate.
- Seven non-binding letters mention 9,000 tonnes/year. Three letters are brands owned by one group; two refer only to dog treats; requested inclusion ranges from 2% to 20%; none contains an accepted specification, completed feeding trial, price, Incoterms or binding volume. No cat-specific buyer has completed technical review.
- Supplier target production cost is USD 4.80/kg at 90% utilization. The pilot line estimates USD 7.20/kg. A chicken by-product meal listing is USD 1.40/kg at 60% crude protein; digestible amino-acid basis, freight, quality and contract terms are unavailable.
- One premium dog-food buyer says informally that up to USD 0.12 per 100 g finished food might be acceptable if palatability, digestibility and a defensible consumer claim are proven. No formula shows the intended PV9 amount per 100 g.
- A dedicated line would add 5,000 tonnes/year nameplate capacity. Qualification yield, saleable output, utilization ramp, working capital and alternative use are unknown.
- Management can fund a 12-week regulatory review and controlled dog-food study. It will not approve the line if the exact legal route is unresolved, if a target-animal study shows persistent intake/stool/clinical issues, or if contribution economics remain negative on a functionally equivalent digestible-protein basis.
