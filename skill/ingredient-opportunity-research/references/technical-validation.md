# Technical and Literature Validation Protocol

Use this protocol for every priority application and for every proposed formulation or validation action.

## Frame the Technical Question

Specify: ingredient identity and grade; product matrix; ingredient function; substitution basis; process steps and thermal/pH/shear conditions; storage and packaging; target outcome; incumbent benchmark; and failure criteria.

## Search and Interpret Evidence

Search in three layers before forming an application conclusion:

1. normalize identity terms and exclusion terms for easily confused ingredients;
2. combine the ingredient with property/mechanism terms;
3. combine the ingredient with the exact product matrix, process, measurement, and failure-mode terms.

Use reviews to build a literature map, then retrieve the original source for every decision-critical formulation, dose, processing, clinical, sensory, or shelf-life claim. If the original cannot be obtained, say that the conclusion is review-mediated rather than silently citing the review as direct evidence.

Prefer exact-matrix evidence in this order:

1. standards and original peer-reviewed studies;
2. patents with worked examples;
3. supplier application studies with disclosed formulation and method;
4. adjacent-matrix studies and mechanistic literature;
5. expert inference.

For each source capture: search query or route; matrix; formulation/dose; control; process; sample size; measurements; result; limitation; conflicts/funding; applicability to the target; and reading depth.

When human evidence affects a decision, also capture the preregistered or stated primary endpoint, whether it was met, attrition, relevant multiplicity or statistical adjustment, adverse events, funding, and author conflicts. Keep safety/tolerance, mechanistic or biomarker changes, patient- or consumer-relevant outcomes, and commercial claims separate. Do not let a positive secondary or exploratory endpoint override a failed primary endpoint.

Run a dedicated safety and tolerability search in addition to the intended-effect search. Derive risk-search terms from the ingredient's identity, mechanism, metabolism, exposure route, dose, target population, regulatory assessments and warnings, known class effects, and signals found during literature review. Search the exact names and synonyms together with safety, tolerability, adverse-event, withdrawal, and dose-response concepts, then expand with the ingredient-specific risk terms identified above. Capture amount, duration, population, event definition, frequency or severity, comparator, and discontinuations. “No adverse effect mentioned” is not equivalent to “no adverse effect occurred.” A regulatory warning or labelling threshold is not automatically a physiological-effect threshold.

Classify reading depth separately from evidence level:

- **F:** original full text and relevant methods/results inspected;
- **A:** original abstract or structured record only;
- **R:** result obtained through a review or secondary citation; original not inspected;
- **P:** patent worked example;
- **S:** supplier or commercially interested application material.

Do not award full-text confidence to an abstract, search snippet, review relay, or supplier statement. For a formal technical recommendation, list any R/A-only claim that could change the decision as a literature gap.

Classify transferability:

- **Direct:** same ingredient, comparable grade, matrix, function, and process.
- **Adjacent:** one material difference; use to design a test, not to claim performance.
- **Mechanistic:** explains a likely effect but does not validate the finished product.
- **Unsupported:** no relevant evidence found.

## Build the Technical Evidence Matrix

Use:

`technical claim | target matrix/process | source matrix/process | key result | transferability | likely failure mode | evidence level | remaining gap`

Explicitly check identity confusion, solubility and dissolution temperature, crystallization or phase behavior, water activity and moisture migration, viscosity/rheology, sweetness or sensory balance, color/Maillard chemistry, pH and thermal stability, interaction with proteins/starch/hydrocolloids/fats, fermentation, shelf life, scale-up, and analytical/label consequences when relevant.

For each promising property, run a polarity check: identify where the property creates value and where the same property could be neutral, harmful, or incompatible with another product requirement. Do not treat a property as universally beneficial.

## Capture Application Cases and Reported Use Amounts

For every priority application, search for concrete cases in the exact or closest available product matrix. Suitable sources include original formulation or processing studies, patents with worked examples, method-disclosed supplier application studies, regulatory or technical dossiers, technical presentations, and disclosed commercial formulation records. A live product label can verify ingredient presence but normally cannot establish the undisclosed use amount.

Capture:

`application/matrix | case/source identity | evidence type | ingredient identity/grade | functional role | formulation basis | reported use amount and original unit | amount basis | relevant co-ingredients | process and conditions | control/benchmark | measured outcome | exact source/locator | reading depth | commercial-status evidence | transferability | limitation`

Preserve the original amount basis, including whether it refers to finished product, dry solids, liquid phase, a subcomponent, replacement fraction, serving, or another denominator. Convert units only when the denominator and assumptions are explicit; show both the original and normalized values.

Separate conclusions:

- **reported case amount:** directly stated in a traceable case;
- **observed amount span:** summarized from comparable cases with the included cases and conditions disclosed;
- **common or typical amount:** use only when the source or a defined, method-disclosed case universe supports representativeness;
- **proposed test amount:** analyst recommendation derived from evidence but not yet validated;
- **commercial amount:** confirmed for the intended formulation or disclosed by an authoritative commercial source.

Do not merge cases with materially different grades, functions, matrices, processes, or amount denominators into one range. If the amount is not disclosed, write `use amount not disclosed`; do not infer it from ingredient-list order, marketing claims, product performance, or a different formulation. A patent or supplier example is a formulation starting point, not proof of common commercial practice.

For substitution or co-formulation cases, additionally capture the functional jobs of the incumbent, total functional-ingredient amount, component amounts and ratio, partner identity/grade, mechanism-relevant environment, order of addition, activation or hydration, process, single-component and incumbent controls, and interaction-analysis method. A combination that performs well is not necessarily synergistic; require an appropriate additive or mixture benchmark before using that term.

## Convert Evidence into Experiments

Recommend an experiment only if it closes a decision-critical gap. State:

`decision | literature-supported starting use range | control and use-level variants | process held constant | measurements | time points | success/failure rule | next decision`

Use a control and the smallest useful use-level or process ladder capable of answering the decision. Keep the regulatory boundary, literature starting range, proposed test amount, and eventual commercial amount distinct. Measure the observed product outcome together with mechanism-aligned variables that can explain success or failure. Use processing, storage, transport, packaging, and use conditions that match the buyer's real chain.

Do not copy a patented worked example into production without IP review. Do not claim clinical, sensory, shelf-life, or cost superiority beyond the tested matrix. If exact evidence is absent, label the recommendation as a hypothesis-driven screening test, not a validated solution.
