# Research Quality Rules

Use these rules before delivering a feasibility report. They formalize what evidence permits what conclusion. They do not impose arbitrary sample counts and must never become an ingredient opportunity score.

For each applicable rule report:

`rule | met/conditional/not met/not applicable | supporting evidence | exact gap | effect on conclusion | next action`

If evidence is inaccessible or the available universe is small, disclose that limitation and narrow the conclusion. Do not fabricate observations or collect low-value data merely to satisfy a count.

## Generalization Rule

- Use completed cases to discover reusable decision questions and evidence boundaries, not to create defaults for other ingredients.
- Derive risk terms, regulatory routes, proposed claims, application categories, technical measurements, price tiers, experiment conditions, sampling plans, and customer-search terms from the current ingredient, product matrix, market, population, and decision.
- Keep only invariant rules across cases: identify the exact material; trace claims to evidence; preserve legal and scientific units; separate permission, feasibility, observed effect, and commercial inference; disclose coverage and uncertainty; define the next validation action.
- Keep named-ingredient examples in test scenarios or example outputs. Do not place them in the generic execution rules unless clearly marked as non-normative illustrations.

## Research Contract

- State ingredient identity, grade/specification, function, geography, downstream scope, business objective, time window, and output depth before drawing conclusions.
- Record supplier grade, capacity, authorizations, production-source restrictions, and evidence package as known or unknown. Unknown supplier inputs make customer priority conditional.
- Do not advance an application while a decision-critical identity conflict remains unresolved.

## Ingredient Properties and Sources

- Every property used in a decision must have an inline retrievable source and locator, test matrix, amount, comparator/method, measured result, reading depth, transferability, limitation, and evidence level.
- Keep the property section limited to measured ingredient properties and risks. Finished-product replacement percentage, formulation performance, sensory acceptance, competitor comparison, adoption, and replacement strategy belong to the application analysis, not the property table.
- Every shortlisted application must trace to a supported property from an original primary source or standard. Review-only, abstract-only, patent-only, supplier-only, adjacent, or mechanistic evidence may justify a test hypothesis, not an unqualified performance claim.
- For every property used to advance an application, test both where it creates value and where it may be neutral, harmful, or fail.
- When human/use exposure matters, run a dedicated tolerability and adverse-effect search and report relevant positive, null, adverse, withdrawal, and dose-response findings found.

## Property-to-Application Map

- Keep only a decision-relevant shortlist; do not fill a preset number when the evidence supports fewer applications.
- Every shortlisted application must show `supported property -> observable problem -> required function -> application`.
- Every shortlisted application must address need, applicable regulation, technical fit, use amount, use cost, and identifiable buyers.
- A decision-critical failed gate means `do not advance`; unresolved regulation means `regulatory unresolved`; adjacent-only technical evidence means an experiment is required or evidence is insufficient.
- For every shortlisted application, identify the incumbent and credible alternative ingredient systems that can perform the same product job. Compare them in the same matrix and on a functionally equivalent basis rather than by ingredient name or equal weight alone.
- Support comparative technical claims with like-for-like evidence. When sources differ materially in matrix, grade, amount, process, endpoint, or conditions, call the comparison directional and require a controlled benchmark before declaring superiority.
- Report the alternatives' advantages and the target ingredient's disadvantages as actively as the target ingredient's strengths. Do not select comparison dimensions only because they favor the target ingredient.
- Decompose the incumbent formulation into its product jobs before recommending full replacement, partial replacement, co-formulation, a process-enabled change, or retaining the incumbent. State which jobs the target ingredient covers and which remain unresolved.
- Support substitution or co-formulation options with traceable formulation evidence that preserves total amount, component amounts/ratio, grades, matrix, mechanism-relevant conditions, process, controls, endpoints, source locator, and transferability.
- Do not label improved combination performance as synergy unless an appropriate additive or mixture benchmark and interaction analysis support that conclusion. Otherwise state that combination performance was observed and synergy remains unverified.
- Present published or supplier formulations as reference options and test starting points, not validated production recipes. Check every option for regulation, label, cost-in-use, supply, processing, sensory, safety, and IP implications.
- For replacement or co-formulation economics, compare incumbent and target ingredient systems on the same finished-product and functional-performance basis. Show retained, removed, reduced, and added components and calculate the ingredient-system cost change.
- When the relevant formulation and comparable price bases are available or transparently estimated, calculate estimated total ingredient cost before and after and show the absolute and percentage change. Keep this separate from packaging, processing, labor, energy, depreciation, logistics, compliance, testing, and total manufacturing cost.

## Product-Format Screening

- Translate every shortlisted application into concrete saleable formats before estimating demand, recommending experiments or selecting customers. A broad benefit, category or use occasion is not a product format.
- For each format, report the product need, ingredient job, matrix/phase, process, contact or consumption mode, pack/dose basis, evidence use amount, regulatory/claim route, alternatives, current SKU adoption, format-level market evidence, buyers and decisive gap.
- Do not include a format merely because the ingredient can be added. Require an observable need, plausible retained function and identifiable buyer market.
- Keep parent-category market, format commercial pool, ingredient-addressable output, ingredient demand and supplier-addressable demand separate.
- Do not allocate parent-market value using an unsupported format share or apply one channel's share to the total market without representativeness evidence.
- Label platform GMV, retail-panel sales, filings, launch counts, units and production volumes by their exact measure and coverage. Filing/launch activity is not sales; bundles cannot be assigned to component formats without a method.
- Do not convert retail value directly to ingredient volume. Show unit volume, average net content, adoption, use amount, active concentration, losses and formulas; otherwise mark ingredient demand not reliably estimable.
- Every priority format requires a format-specific validation matrix, incumbent control, amount ladder, process/packaging conditions, endpoints and success/failure rule.

## Food Regulation and Claims

- Unless the user limits geography, audit China, the United States, and the European Union together.
- Use one combined table in the main report. Give each jurisdiction-and-category combination its own row when use levels or conditions differ.
- Every regulatory row must state legal route/status, exact category/use, regulator's original use level/unit or GMP/quantum satis, restrictions/warnings/supplier applicability, application result, primary source/version, and access date.
- For China food-additive work, check the current GB 2760 text, including GB 2760-2024 while applicable, and other possible legal routes. Foodmate is an accepted retrieval source.
- Every proposed label or marketing claim must have a finished-product eligibility check covering threshold, conditions, evidence needed, qualifiers/warnings, and jurisdiction. Permission to claim is not efficacy evidence.
- Do not mark a jurisdiction `pass` when the exact identity/grade, category, population, use amount, production source/strain, or supplier reliance remains unresolved.

## Raw-Material Price Evidence

- Every price observation must identify ingredient/specification, quantity, supplier type/source, commercial terms available, currency/unit, observation date, and evidence band.
- Call a range executable only when matched, current quotations are sufficiently comparable and independent for the intended buying decision. Otherwise call them limited quotations or public signals and show the observations separately.
- A transaction is a benchmark only for its stated terms; it is not automatically the market price.
- Report the observation count, range or individual values, central tendency when meaningful, dates, and terms. Investigate material outliers using identity, grade, quantity, geography, tax, freight, and channel differences rather than an arbitrary percentage cutoff.
- Refresh prices when their age or market volatility could change the decision. State the refresh basis instead of using one universal expiry period.
- Support a price trend with comparable time-series observations. Unmatched routes, grades, natural-status claims, quantities, geographies or terms support price dispersion only, not a calculated decline.

## Market Size and Demand Evidence

- Define the exact measure, ingredient identity/grade, geography, period, unit and value-chain level before reporting market size or growth. Keep capacity, production, shipments, sales volume, consumption/demand volume, inventory and sales value separate.
- Triangulate supply, trade and downstream-use views when the number affects the decision. If one view is unavailable, disclose it and narrow the conclusion rather than substituting an opaque report.
- Capacity is not production or demand; planned capacity and sales targets are not actual sales. Flexible multi-product capacity requires an allocation basis.
- Confirm whether customs codes uniquely identify the ingredient. When they do not, search names, CAS numbers, trade names and counterparties at shipment level and disclose residual contamination and missing domestic trade.
- A downstream estimate must show finished-product output, adoption share, inclusion rate, active concentration, losses and formulas. Ingredient-list order does not establish inclusion rate.
- Trace repeated figures to their earliest discoverable source. Sources sharing the same unattributed number or assumptions are one evidence chain, not independent corroboration.
- Reconcile disagreement through scope, grade, route, period, geography, price level, concentration, inventory, re-export, utilization and source incentives. Do not average incompatible estimates.
- Check that volume, comparable realized price and value are arithmetically coherent and compatible with supply capacity and downstream use. Quarantine unexplained order-of-magnitude contradictions.
- Classify the result as verified estimate, corroborated range, directional range, or not reliably estimable. Show base year, access date, volume/value separately, formulas, sensitivity, exclusions and next evidence needed.
- A forecast requires a traceable base value, period, method and scenario assumptions. TAM must be narrowed for regulation, saleable grade, geography, application fit and plausible adoption before it becomes supplier-addressable demand.
- When shortage, unmet demand, low penetration, substitution or new capacity supports the opportunity conclusion, apply the demand-maturity, supply-constraint and evidence-status axes plus the gap-specific alignment fields in [market-size-and-demand.md](market-size-and-demand.md). Treat non-binding RFQs, LOIs, interviews and price interest as stated buyer interest rather than committed demand.
- Calculate a commercial gap only from aligned effective supply and demand using non-overlapping components. Otherwise report the applicable latent need, directional mismatch, scenario whitespace or `not reliably estimable` conclusion from [market-size-and-demand.md](market-size-and-demand.md).
- Treat two matching or arithmetically compatible market numbers as corroboration only after verifying independent provenance and aligned definitions; repeated or producer-seeded estimates remain one evidence chain.
- When supplier financial relevance affects the decision, show standalone scenario revenue from saleable volume, realized price and utilization or sell-through, compare it with the relevant business denominator, and keep the result separate from a forecast.
- When platform or portfolio value affects the decision, identify the exact reusable assets and named adjacent products, then separate demonstrated reuse from strategic hypothesis. Do not assign a portfolio revenue scale without product-level demand and economics.
- Derive scale-up triggers from supplier economics, qualification lead time, committed or probability-weighted demand, flexible-capacity alternatives and downside tolerance. Label unevidenced trigger percentages as management choices, not research findings.

## Use Amount and Use Cost

- For every priority application, distinguish regulatory boundary, evidence starting amount, proposed test amount, and commercial intended amount/status.
- Show finished-product or subcomponent basis, normalized raw-material price/band, yield/loss, cost per kg finished product, cost per saleable unit, formula, assumptions, excluded costs, and confidence/gap.
- Run sensitivity scenarios when uncertainty in price, use amount, yield, or subcomponent weight could change the decision. Choose scenario count and bounds from the evidence rather than a preset template.
- Never treat daily intake or warning threshold as a formulation maximum; never call ingredient-use cost total BOM without complete BOM and conversion costs.
- For every priority application, search traceable exact- or adjacent-matrix application cases and preserve ingredient grade, functional role, formulation basis, original use amount/unit, amount denominator, process, control, measured outcome, source locator, and transferability.
- Distinguish a directly reported case amount, an observed span across disclosed comparable cases, a source-supported common/typical amount, a proposed test amount, and a confirmed commercial amount. Do not present one case or a mixed set of incomparable cases as normal industry usage.
- Treat an undisclosed amount as unknown. Ingredient-list order, product claims, observed performance, and unrelated formulations do not justify back-calculating the amount.

## Technical and Literature Validation

- Every decision-critical technical recommendation needs an original exact-matrix source. If only adjacent or mechanistic evidence exists, label it a screening hypothesis.
- Capture identity/grade, matrix, amount, control, process, sample size where applicable, measurements, result, limitation, conflicts/funding, transferability, and reading depth for each decision-critical source.
- Every experiment must include an appropriate control and the smallest justified variant ladder capable of answering the decision. Do not impose a fixed number of variants.
- Storage or shelf-life work must include a baseline and decision-relevant time points. Accelerated-only evidence cannot establish real-time shelf life.
- State measurements, time points, and observable success/failure rules before recommending an experiment.

## Product and Company Adoption

- Define a reproducible company/SKU universe, inclusion rule, geography, channels, and observation window before interpreting adoption.
- Report companies screened, relevant live SKUs found, complete labels checked, inaccessible labels, and coverage limitations.
- Every `verified current use` conclusion must have A1 or A2 evidence tied to the exact SKU, market, formulation/version where known, and observation date.
- A `no public evidence found` conclusion must describe official, retail, registry, or label-database channels actually checked. Weak coverage narrows the conclusion; it does not prove company-wide absence.
- Corroborate positive label evidence with another current source when practical; otherwise state single-source risk.
- Never aggregate SKU absence into company absence. Patents, supplier logos, titles, claims, snippets, reviews, and old launch pages are development or lead evidence only.

## Effects, Consumer Evidence, and Commercial Evidence

- Separate technical effect, human/use effect and adverse effects, regulatory claim eligibility, consumer evidence, and commercial evidence. Use `no evidence found` when a channel was searched but unsupported.
- An affirmative consumer or commercial conclusion must satisfy every SMART element. Otherwise label it a directional signal and state the missing elements.
- Choose review sample size from the decision, accessible universe, product diversity, and saturation of themes. Always disclose the universe, sample, selection/coding method, denominator, date window, and positive and negative themes. Do not claim representativeness from convenience samples.
- A trend conclusion requires comparable observations over time and a source/method capable of supporting change. A single-period observation is a snapshot.
- Ratings, reviews, sales, GMV, distribution, listing duration, SKU expansion, and brand investment cannot establish ingredient causality without a valid causal design.

## Market Awareness and Education — Conditional Module

- Apply this module only when awareness or education could materially change positioning, channel, claim comprehension, launch cost, customer adoption, or the opportunity decision. Novelty or unfamiliarity alone does not require a standalone section.
- When applied, separately assess name recognition, benefit-concept recognition, accuracy of understanding, category association, trust/concern and claim comprehension for the defined audience, market, category and period.
- Keep consumer awareness separate from brand, formulator, regulator, clinician and procurement awareness. Do not convert supplier-created content volume or professional familiarity into consumer understanding.
- Every awareness conclusion must disclose the signal, method, universe or sample, prompted versus spontaneous measurement, geography, channel, period and limitation. Search volume, content volume and product count are directional signals, not awareness percentages.
- Classify the education burden by explicit evidence as established ingredient-led understanding, benefit known but ingredient name unfamiliar, emerging and explanation-dependent, misunderstood or trust-constrained, or no adequate evidence.
- Convert the result into a go-to-market choice: ingredient-led, benefit-led without ingredient education, behind-the-label, or targeted professional education. State the minimum message, permitted-claim fit, likely education carrier, required evidence assets and next message/concept test.
- Low awareness is not an automatic rejection and high awareness is not proof of correct understanding, trust, purchase intent or commercial success.

## Food Terminology and Target-Language Quality

- For Chinese or bilingual food reports, identify the researched ingredient unambiguously and normalize decision-critical terms internally using authoritative regulatory, standards and scientific usage before drafting.
- Preserve distinctions among molecular family, molecule, form, production source, commercial grade, process, technical function, physiological outcome, legal category and marketing claim. Do not translate one into another for fluency.
- Preserve foreign legal categories and official claim wording; explain them in Chinese without silently replacing them with a superficially similar Chinese legal concept.
- Run a separate target-language audit for identity consistency, regulatory and technical meaning, claim strength, natural syntax and source retrievability. If no authoritative Chinese equivalent exists, retain the original term, define it and mark the translation provisional.
- Do not output a standalone terminology table by default. Include one only when ambiguity among names, family members, grades, sources, legal categories, or claims could change the decision.

## Potential Customers and KA Priority

- Deliver the account count requested by the user. If no count is specified, choose a manageable list and state the selection rule rather than applying a universal default.
- Every account must include business unit/brand, relevant product, geography, account-specific fit evidence, observed adoption status/evidence class, buying/development signal, priority rule, source/date, coverage gap, and next verification action.
- Preserve SKU-level evidence before company aggregation. Keep adoption status separate from `plausible buyer` or `exploratory lead` qualification.
- Assign P1 only when current evidence supports a near-term reason to engage, technical and regulatory fit, supplier-product fit, and a reachable buying path. Material unresolved issues lower the priority and must be named; do not use a fixed uncertainty count.
- Evaluate growth at the business unit, category, brand, product family, geography, or channel most directly connected to potential ingredient demand. Use company-wide growth only as an indirect signal unless that linkage is evidenced.
- State the growth metric, scope, period, comparison basis, source/date, and disclosed drivers. Separate organic demand from price/mix, acquisition, currency, promotion, distribution, and low-base effects when the evidence permits.
- Use relevant growth as a priority modifier after technical, regulatory, supplier, and access fit—not as proof of ingredient demand or purchase intent. Report counter-risks such as supply, qualification, margin, execution, or growth-sustainability pressure.
- Name individuals only with current public or user-provided role evidence. Never infer confidential projects, incumbent supplier, purchase volume, contract price, satisfaction, or decision authority.

## Final Report Integrity

- Every decision-critical claim must carry an appropriate source, date where time-sensitive, evidence level, and limitation.
- Distinguish facts, estimates, hypotheses, and unknowns in every decision-critical section.
- Verify every number against its source before finalizing: unit (亿/万/十亿/billion/MM), order of magnitude, denominator, period and identity; re-transcribe any figure taken from a source; treat a factor-of-10, unit, denominator or period error in a decision-critical figure as a hard defect even when the conclusion is unchanged.
- End with the rule-based completeness check, unmet or conditional rules, validation actions, and decisions reserved for supplier, regulatory, or industry experts.
- Do not issue an overall model-authored opportunity grade. The separate rubric in [test-scenarios.md](test-scenarios.md) evaluates report quality only.

## Decision-Readiness Blockers

The report is not decision-ready when it relies on any of the following:

- wrong or unresolved ingredient identity;
- a shortlisted application without applicable primary-source regulation for a required market;
- an application recommendation without a sourced property trace or explicit hypothesis label;
- a company-use claim without qualifying SKU-level or explicitly scoped company-level evidence;
- a regulatory claim presented as proof of efficacy;
- sales, reviews, claims, or patents presented as ingredient-causal effect;
- price called a market or transaction price without appropriate evidence band and terms;
- use cost without use amount, finished-product basis, price basis, and formula;
- a human/use conclusion that omits foreseeable adverse effects;
- potential-customer status without source date and disclosed search/SKU coverage.
- a decision-critical market-size or growth claim that conflates capacity, production, demand or sales value, lacks a defined base year/method, or relies on an opaque/repeated estimate without disclosed uncertainty;
- a shortage, unmet-demand, low-penetration, substitution or capacity claim presented as a commercial supply-demand gap without separate demand-maturity, supply-constraint and evidence-status classifications, gap-relevant aligned inputs, non-overlapping calculation and invalidation/next-evidence condition;
- non-binding RFQs, LOIs, interviews, price interest, low penetration or an incumbent pool presented as committed unmet demand;
- a shortlisted application that remains a broad category without a concrete product-format screen, or whose format retail value is presented as ingredient-addressable demand without adoption and use-amount evidence;
- a consumer-facing recommendation whose success materially depends on ingredient recognition, trust, or claim comprehension but does not assess that dependency;
- a Chinese or bilingual report whose decision-critical terminology is inconsistent, stronger than the source, or not traceable to the original term;
- a decision-critical number transcribed from a source with a factor-of-10, unit, denominator or period error (for example, 200 million written as 两亿件 for two billion units).
