# Food Terminology and Language Protocol

Use this protocol for Chinese or bilingual food-ingredient deliverables. The objective is technically natural target-language writing, not literal sentence-by-sentence translation. For non-food categories (beauty, household, pet care), apply the same principles with that category's terminology sources (INCI, cosmetic catalogue, pet-food standards) in place of the food-specific examples below.

## Establish Identity and Working Terminology

Before drafting, identify the researched ingredient unambiguously: preferred local name, English name, abbreviation when relevant, exact identity/grade/source, and the easily confused substance that must be excluded. Maintain a working terminology table internally only when needed:

`concept/entity | preferred Chinese term | English term | abbreviation | exact identity or scope | authoritative usage source | accepted synonym | misleading or forbidden rendering | report usage note`

Prioritize terminology found in current Chinese regulations and standards, regulator publications, Chinese Pharmacopoeia or relevant industry standards, authoritative Chinese textbooks or dictionaries, and peer-reviewed Chinese literature. Preserve the original legal term when discussing a foreign authorization and explain its Chinese meaning instead of silently replacing it with the nearest Chinese regulatory concept.

Distinguish molecular family, individual molecule, salt or form, production organism or source, commercial grade, process, mechanism, technical function, physiological outcome, product claim, and marketing expression. Similar-looking terms are not interchangeable.

## Identity Traps That Change the Decision

These patterns have produced identity errors in practice; verify each before writing:

- **Family versus molecule:** 维生素 B6 (vitamin B6) is a family label; the molecule may be 吡哆醇 (pyridoxine), 吡哆醛 (pyridoxal), 吡哆胺 (pyridoxamine), or 磷酸吡哆醛 (pyridoxal 5′-phosphate, PLP). Do not let a family name stand in for the researched grade.
- **Sugars and sugar alcohols:** 异麦芽酮糖醇 (isomalt / isomaltitol, INS/E 953) is not 异麦芽酮糖 (isomaltulose); 山梨糖醇/山梨醇 (sorbitol) is not 山梨酸钾 (potassium sorbate, a preservative); 木糖醇 (xylitol) is not 木糖 (xylose). One-character differences change identity, regulation and claims.
- **Chemical name versus INCI versus trade name:** the same molecule can appear as a chemical name, an INCI name, an E number, a CAS number, and a brand name (e.g., 益寿糖 for isomalt). Record which identifier was used for each regulatory check and do not swap them silently.
- **Source/strain specificity:** 透明质酸钠 (sodium hyaluronate) may be microbial fermentation, animal-derived, or of different molecular weights; a strain-specific or source-specific authorization applies only to the exact product, not the same-named molecule.
- **Regulated categories with different Chinese scopes:** a term permitted in 化妆品 (cosmetics) may not map to 食品 (food) usage; 营养强化剂 (nutrition fortifier) is a distinct legal category from 食品添加剂 (food additive) in GB 14880 versus GB 2760, and neither equals a 新食品原料 (novel food ingredient) route.

## Cross-Jurisdiction Terminology

- China: cite the exact GB/GB 2760/GB 14880 category name, the 食品类别 (food category) and use level; use the terminology of the current standard, not a paraphrase.
- United States: cite the CFR section (e.g., 21 CFR 172.515), GRAS status, or FDA food-contact notification; do not translate "GRAS" as if it were a Chinese approval.
- European Union: cite the E number or the EU additive regulation (Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008) category and conditions; preserve the E-number rather than inventing a Chinese equivalent.
- When a foreign authorization has no Chinese equivalent, preserve the original legal term and explain its meaning; do not silently map it to the nearest Chinese regulatory concept.

## False Friends and Literal-Translation Traps

- Do not translate an English modifier into a stronger Chinese conclusion. Keep association versus causation, tolerance versus safety, intended use versus authorized use, mechanism versus demonstrated outcome, and "contains" versus "is effective".
- 天然 (natural) is a claim with legal weight in some jurisdictions; do not attach it to a molecule unless the source and process support it.
- 低糖/无糖 (low sugar / no sugar) and 无添加糖 (no added sugar) have different legal thresholds in GB 28050; do not use them interchangeably, and never equate 无蔗糖 (no sucrose) with 无糖 (no sugar).
- "Sugar-free" does not mean carbohydrate-free; "0 糖" does not mean 0 热量. Preserve the claim scope.
- 有助于 (contributes to), 可降低 (may reduce), 维持 (maintains) and 治疗 (treats) sit on different legal and scientific levels; translate the evidence, not the marketing intention.
- Do not render a proprietary or branded ingredient as a generic name, and do not render a generic molecule as a brand.

## Units, Denominators and Numbers

- Preserve the denominator exactly: mg/100 g versus mg/kg versus mg per serving versus percent; do not normalize silently between 中国 (per 100 g) and other jurisdictions without stating the conversion.
- Preserve the reference basis: per product, per serving, per day, per body weight (mg/kg bw/day), and per unit of finished product are different measures.
- In bilingual output, every number, unit, percentage, date and company/SKU identifier must match exactly between languages; state the currency and price basis in both.
- Analytical endpoints and methods (e.g., 膳食纤维 AOAC 991.43) must keep their official method reference, not a colloquial description.

## Transliteration and Naming Policy

- On first use, write the preferred Chinese term followed by the English term and abbreviation when this improves auditability. Thereafter use one stable term.
- Prefer established transliterations found in standards and regulator publications over ad-hoc phonetic transliterations. When no authoritative Chinese equivalent exists, retain the original term, provide a short operational definition, and label the translation provisional.
- Keep article and regulation titles in their original language when needed for retrieval; a Chinese explanatory title may follow in parentheses.
- Do not use English sentence order, stacked noun phrases, unexplained abbreviations, or abstract verbs that make Chinese read like machine translation. Rewrite the paragraph around the decision question in natural professional Chinese.

## Final Language Audit

Run a separate target-language editing pass after the evidence and conclusions are fixed:

1. identity consistency: one entity is not given multiple unexplained names, and the researched grade is named identically in the title, conclusion and tables;
2. regulatory consistency: official categories, claims and legal effects are not colloquially paraphrased, and foreign authorizations keep their original legal terms;
3. technical consistency: matrix, process, use amount, endpoint and effect terms retain their scientific meaning;
4. claim-strength consistency: the Chinese wording is no stronger than the source evidence, and claim words sit at the correct level of the evidence ladder;
5. numeric consistency (bilingual): numbers, units, denominators, percentages, dates and identifiers match exactly across languages;
6. readability: each sentence has a clear subject and decision purpose, and literal English syntax is removed;
7. retrieval: decision-critical translated terms remain traceable to the original source term.

## When to Show a Terminology Table

The default report should contain one concise ingredient-identity statement, not a standalone terminology section. Show a terminology table only when multiple names, translations, molecular family members, grades, sources, legal categories, or claims could materially change identity, regulatory status, evidence transfer, or the decision. If terminology remains disputed or market-specific, show the alternatives and state which one the report adopts. Do not hide uncertainty behind fluent wording.
