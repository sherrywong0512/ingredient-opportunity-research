# Frozen synthetic evidence pack: MycoPro-PV9 pet-food scale decision

## Decision request

A fermentation company is deciding whether to approve a 5,000-tonne/year line for `MycoPro-PV9` as a protein ingredient for complete dog and cat food in China and the United States. Prepare a Chinese decision memo of no more than 1,200 Chinese characters and recommend one option:

- `approve scale-up`;
- `run a bounded dog-food validation`;
- `stop the pet-food project`.

Use only this frozen evidence pack. Do not browse or add facts.

## Evidence available at the cutoff

- **Exact product:** `MycoPro-PV9` is heat-inactivated biomass from the fictional fungal strain `Paecilomyces variotii PV9`, produced on a defined sugar substrate. Pilot material is 65% crude protein on a dry basis. Amino-acid digestibility, residual nucleic acids, mycotoxin panel, batch variability and kill-step validation are not provided.
- **Adjacent published study:** a 28-day adult-dog study tested biomass from strain `P. variotii KCL-24`, not PV9, at 4% and 8% inclusion in an extruded diet. The pack reports similar apparent total-tract protein digestibility at 4%, lower voluntary intake during days 1–4 at 8%, and no serious adverse event. It did not test cats, growth, reproduction, allergy, long-term use, full protein replacement or PV9.
- **Supplier application sheet:** an internal PV9 bench test replaced 20% of chicken by-product meal by weight in one dog-kibble formula. It reports acceptable extrusion appearance but contains no control diet, palatability, digestibility, amino-acid adequacy, stool, bloodwork, shelf-life or statistical analysis.
- **Cat suitability:** no PV9 or KCL-24 cat feeding study is available. The pack contains no data on taurine, arginine, sulfur amino acids, arachidonic acid or preformed vitamin A adequacy in a complete cat diet.
- **United States route:** a public GRAS notice covers fictional strain `P. variotii PV7` for specified human foods. It does not name PV9, animal food or complete pet food. The pack contains no FDA animal-food consultation, AAFCO definition or state registration for PV9.
- **China route:** a search note says the exact name `PV9 fungal biomass` was not found in the provided excerpt of the Feed Materials Catalogue. The excerpt is incomplete and absence from it is not proof of prohibition. No exact-product approval or legal opinion is provided.
- **Market signal:** a report values the global pet-food market at USD 140 billion. It gives no complete-food output, protein-system share, adoption rate or PV9 inclusion rate.
- **Buyer signal:** seven non-binding letters mention 9,000 tonnes/year. Three letters are brands owned by one group; two refer only to dog treats; requested inclusion ranges from 2% to 20%; none contains an accepted specification, completed feeding trial, price, Incoterms or binding volume. No cat-specific buyer has completed technical review.
- **Economics:** supplier target production cost is USD 4.80/kg at 90% utilization. The pilot line currently estimates USD 7.20/kg. A chicken by-product meal listing is USD 1.40/kg at 60% crude protein; digestible amino-acid basis, freight, quality and contract terms are unavailable. One premium dog-food buyer says informally that up to USD 0.12 per 100 g finished food might be acceptable if palatability, digestibility and a defensible consumer claim are proven. No formula shows the intended PV9 amount per 100 g.
- **Capacity:** the proposed line is dedicated and adds 5,000 tonnes/year nameplate capacity. Qualification yield, saleable output, utilization ramp, working capital and alternative use are unknown.

## Constraints

- Management can fund a 12-week regulatory review and controlled dog-food study before a scale decision.
- Management will not approve the line if the exact legal route is unresolved, if a target-animal study shows persistent intake/stool/clinical issues, or if contribution economics remain negative on a functionally equivalent digestible-protein basis.
- The decision memo must distinguish exact-product evidence from strain, species and formulation transfer.
