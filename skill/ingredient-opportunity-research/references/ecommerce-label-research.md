# E-commerce Ingredient-Label Research Protocol

When current product adoption, competitor formulations, substitutes, product claims, or potential-customer status depends on retail SKU evidence → use this protocol.

## Define Coverage

Before searching, state:

`target geography | category and subcategory | companies/brands | SKU inclusion rule | platforms | observation window | ingredient identity/synonyms/exclusions`

Search every priority company's relevant live SKU universe when practical. Otherwise define a reproducible shortlist from the target application, product function, formulation problem, claim set, market, and current availability. Do not select only products that contain the target ingredient.

## Search Platforms in Evidence Order

### China

1. Tmall official brand flagship stores and JD brand-operated, official flagship, or self-operated stores;
2. major retailers such as Freshippo, Sam's Club, Metro, and brand-authorized grocery channels;
3. verified enterprise stores on Taobao and other platforms;
4. Pinduoduo, Douyin shops, ordinary sellers, and social commerce for discovery only unless the current package label is legible and attributable.

### International

1. official brand sites and direct stores;
2. Amazon grocery pages, Walmart, Target, Instacart, iHerb, Tesco, Sainsbury's, Ocado, Carrefour, and other major retailers appropriate to the market;
3. local marketplaces and distributors for discovery or corroboration.

Platform text may be seller-supplied. Prefer a legible on-pack ingredient image tied to the exact SKU, then corroborate it with an official page, authorized retailer, product registry, or second current channel.

## Inspect Each SKU

1. Confirm brand, exact SKU, flavor/variant, pack size, target market, store identity, and current availability.
2. Inspect structured `Ingredients` or `配料表` fields, product parameters, all packaging images, detail-page images, and authorized customer-service label images.
3. Transcribe the complete ingredient list verbatim; do not record only the target keyword.
4. Search the normalized ingredient name, local-language names, English names, E/INS number, branded names, synonyms, spelling/OCR variants, and explicitly exclude easily confused substances.
5. Record label or page version/date when visible. If no version is visible, record only the observation date and mark formulation currency uncertain.
6. Save the URL, store type, screenshot or image reference when authorized, and any corroborating source.

Use this capture table:

`platform | store type | company/brand | exact SKU/variant | market | pack size | current availability | complete ingredient-list access | exact target-ingredient wording or substitute | label/page date | observation date | URL/evidence capture | corroboration | adoption class | limitation`

## Conclude Conservatively

- A current legible package label tied to an exact SKU can support A1 under the adoption ladder.
- A complete official product-page ingredient list corroborated by an authorized retail listing can support A2.
- A marketplace title, benefit claim, search snippet, seller description, review, or front-of-pack statement alone is L1.
- A current complete label that omits the ingredient supports `confirmed absent` only for that SKU, market, and observed formulation—not for the company.
- If the label is inaccessible, write `label not accessible`; do not convert it to `no use`.

Report coverage:

`companies screened | relevant live SKUs found | complete labels checked | labels inaccessible | platforms/markets | observation dates | verified users | substitutes | no-public-evidence results`

Keep adoption evidence separate from sales evidence. Price, sales count, reviews, rankings, and current availability can describe commercial presence, but do not prove the ingredient unless the SKU label does.

Use only public pages, authorized accounts, and browser sessions the user is permitted to access. Do not bypass access controls or purchase credentials.
