# Customer Discovery and Sales Deliverables

## Account Qualification

Classify account status precisely:

- **Verified current user:** direct, current evidence names the ingredient or a clearly identified branded equivalent.
- **Plausible buyer:** relevant product/application and buying need are evidenced, but current ingredient use is not.
- **Exploratory lead:** strategic or technical fit is plausible but evidence is incomplete.

Assign priority bands with transparent rules:

- **P1:** strong application fit, active product/innovation signal, target geography, reachable buying path, supplier product/authorization/evidence-package fit, and no visible hard blocker.
- **P2:** clear fit, but a material uncertainty in timing, regulation, scale, supplier fit, or access prevents near-term prioritization.
- **P3:** hypothesis worth testing, but current evidence is insufficient for active pursuit.

Never rank an account solely by company size or brand fame.

### Growth and Momentum as a Priority Modifier

Evaluate growth at the level most directly connected to ingredient demand:

1. the relevant business unit, category, brand, product family, geography, or channel;
2. the company's overall growth only when the relevant business contribution or strategic linkage is evidenced;
3. product and investment momentum when financial segmentation is unavailable, using launches, SKU expansion, distribution, capacity, R&D hiring, regulatory activity, or other method-disclosed signals.

Record the metric definition, reporting scope, geography/channel, period and comparison basis, source/date, and whether the observed change reflects organic volume, price/mix, acquisition, currency, promotion, distribution expansion, or a low-base effect when disclosed. Do not combine revenue, volume, GMV, rankings, review velocity, and company valuation into one growth claim.

Use relevant growth to refine timing and priority only after application fit, regulation, supplier-product fit, and buying path have passed. Growth in an unrelated unit must not raise priority. Company-wide growth without segment evidence is an indirect signal. A fast-growing relevant business can justify earlier engagement because potential demand, budget, launch cadence, or urgency may be increasing, but also check supply assurance, qualification capacity, margin pressure, operational strain, and sustainability of growth.

Use this table:

`company | relevant business unit/category/brand | target product link | growth or momentum metric | scope/geography/channel | period/comparison basis | observed direction and driver | evidence/source date | directness to ingredient demand | priority implication | counter-risk | limitation/next check`

Before ranking, map the supplier's saleable grade or molecule, capacity, target-jurisdiction authorization, production source or strain restrictions, protected-use constraints, and available quality, regulatory, technical, and clinical evidence. If these inputs are unknown, mark the ranking conditional; do not name one universal first account.

Before ranking, state the account universe and the basis for “leading”: comparable sales/market share, distribution reach, category leadership, product innovation, or another explicit rule. If the list uses mixed bases, label it a strategic target list rather than a market ranking.

For current-use research, record SKU verification coverage:

`company | relevant SKU universe | SKUs/labels checked | channel and date | labels accessible | adoption result | remaining coverage gap`

`No public evidence found` is valid only within the disclosed coverage; it is not proof of non-use.

Apply the adoption-evidence ladder in [feature-application-adoption.md](feature-application-adoption.md). Do not classify a potential customer as a verified current user from a patent, supplier logo, distributor page, marketplace title, search snippet, or general category membership.

Apply [customer-adoption-search.md](customer-adoption-search.md) to every shortlisted account. Preserve the SKU-level rows before aggregating to company status, and keep observed adoption separate from buyer-fit inference.

When e-commerce labels are relevant, apply [ecommerce-label-research.md](ecommerce-label-research.md) before assigning current-use status or using substitute formulations to qualify an account.

## Potential-Customer Table

For each account include:

`company | business unit/brand | relevant products and SKU coverage | geography | why the ingredient may matter | observed adoption status and evidence class | verified substitute/development evidence | relevant-unit growth/momentum evidence | buying/innovation signal | likely initiating/influencing/deciding/executing roles | priority band and rule | sources/dates and coverage gap | next validation step`

## Interview Guide

Generate only when requested. Tailor questions to the knowledge gap and respondent role. Cover:

1. current formulation/application and desired outcome;
2. unmet technical, sensory, cost, supply, or claim problems;
3. incumbent solution and switching friction;
4. specification, evidence, quality, regulatory, and documentation requirements;
5. acceptable economics and value-in-use logic without pressuring for confidential figures;
6. project stage, evaluation process, stakeholders, timeline, and next evidence needed.

Avoid leading questions that merely validate the supplier's preferred narrative.

## KA Attack Card

Create only after an account is selected or the user requests prioritization.

```markdown
# KA Attack Card — [Account]

## Account thesis
- Evidence-backed reason this account may buy
- Account status and priority-band rule
- Biggest uncertainty

## Relevant business and product map
| Business/brand | Product/application | Geography | Evidence | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Decision map
| Role | Function/person if verified | Likely concern | Evidence/status | Access path |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## One primary value proposition
- Customer problem
- Supplier value
- Proof required
- Likely objection and response

## 30/60/90-day sequence
| Window | Objective | Action/material | Success signal | Exit/escalation condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
```

Do not infer confidential projects, supplier satisfaction, pricing, or decision authority from job titles alone.

## Presentation Outline

Generate only when requested and anchor every slide to the completed feasibility report:

1. decision and scope;
2. downstream application map;
3. evidence supporting shortlisted applications;
4. products, competition, and substitutes;
5. technical/regulatory constraints;
6. customer map and priority rules;
7. recommended validation or KA actions;
8. risks, gaps, and decisions required.

Use the language requested by the user. For bilingual slides, keep terminology consistent and avoid doubling slide density.
