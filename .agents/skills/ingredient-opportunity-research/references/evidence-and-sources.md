# Evidence and Source Protocol

## Evidence Levels

Assign an evidence level to every material claim. The level describes the evidence, not whether the commercial opportunity is attractive.

| Level | Definition | Examples | Permitted wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Direct, current, reproducible primary evidence | regulator or standards text; paper or patent; company filing; official product page; authorized raw platform export | `confirmed by...` within the stated scope |
| E2 | Strong primary observation or multiple consistent high-quality sources | official brand/product evidence combined with platform observation; several independent sources agreeing | `well supported` |
| E3 | Credible secondary evidence with a disclosed method | reputable industry report, trade publication, database estimate | `reported/estimated by...` |
| E4 | Directional signal with limited reproducibility | marketplace plug-in estimate, search trend, review sample, distributor listing | `directional signal` |
| E5 | Analyst inference or unverified lead | inferred formulation fit, likely buyer role, unsupported market rumor | `hypothesis to validate` |

For each important claim, record: `claim | evidence | exact source/URL or DOI | source locator | source date | access date | geography/channel | metric definition | evidence level | reading depth when literature | limitation`.

For ingredient properties, cite the source inside the same table row. Give enough information to retrieve and audit it: first author or issuing body, year, short title, journal/standard, DOI or stable URL, and page/table/figure/section when available. A bibliography-only citation is insufficient because it obscures which source supports which property.

Use one property claim per row. If several sources support a claim, identify which provides the direct measurement and which only corroborates it. If one paper reports several conditions, preserve the condition tied to the quoted result rather than extracting a context-free number.

Keep source reliability separate from claim confidence. Several low-quality sources repeating one claim do not raise its evidence level.

Treat numerical agreement as corroboration only after tracing source independence and aligning the metric, identity, grade, geography, period, value-chain level, and price basis. A producer estimate and a review or market report that repeats or is arithmetically compatible with that estimate remain one evidence chain until independence is shown.

Decompose compound company claims before assigning confidence. Verify production route, purity, scale, capacity, customer qualification, order receipt, current sales, and financial contribution as separate claims; a filing that supports one part does not confirm the others. Treat company websites, investor replies, speeches, and public-account articles as attributable company statements unless an audited filing, permit, customer evidence, transaction, or reproducible technical record corroborates the exact claim.

For literature, reading depth is not evidence quality. A full supplier brochure remains interested evidence; an abstract of a strong randomized trial remains abstract-only. Report both dimensions.

Do not write `studies show`, `research indicates`, or an uncited property adjective such as `stable`, `low hygroscopicity`, or `well tolerated`. Replace it with the measured observation and its test conditions, or label it `unverified claim`.

## Source Order

Prefer sources in this order, adjusting for the question:

1. regulators, standards bodies, peer-reviewed papers, patents, and official statistical data;
2. company filings, official corporate/product sites, authorized retailer or marketplace pages;
3. method-disclosed industry databases and research reports;
4. trade media, distributor catalogs, search trends, social and review data;
5. analyst inference.

Use current sources for prices, product availability, regulations, personnel, market rankings, and company activity. Record the access date.

## Paid-Data and Public-Source Paths

### With authorized data tools

- Export raw observations where possible.
- Preserve tool name, query, filters, geography, time window, metric definition, and extraction date.
- Treat third-party sales estimates as estimates unless the provider documents otherwise.

### Without paid data

Triangulate instead of fabricating precision:

- product presence and launch activity from official sites and retailers;
- relative assortment, review velocity, price history, and ranking signals from marketplaces;
- demand language and pain points from a disclosed review/content sample;
- company scale and strategy from filings, press releases, job postings, and factory/OEM evidence;
- regulation and science from official texts and original studies;
- interviews or expert checks to close the highest-impact gaps.

State which conclusions cannot be supported without paid or proprietary data.

## Minimum Sampling Discipline

Before writing a consumer or commercial conclusion, apply a SMART evidence audit:

- **Specific:** exact object, market/channel, population/customer, and outcome;
- **Measurable:** metric, unit, sample/denominator, and comparator or baseline;
- **Auditable and attributable:** reproducible source/method and an explicit attribution boundary;
- **Relevant:** match to the target application and decision;
- **Time-bound:** observation/study period and access date.

For reviews, disclose platform, exact SKU, sample size, date window, selection method, coding method, denominator, positive themes, negative themes, and inaccessible data. For sales or market signals, disclose metric definition, source or estimation method, geography/channel, period, baseline/comparator, promotion dependence, and seasonality. If these fields are unavailable, state the exact gap and narrow the conclusion. Reviews, ratings, sales, growth, listing duration, and marketing claims are observational signals and cannot by themselves prove that the ingredient caused the outcome.

- Define the universe, inclusion rule, geography, channel, and time window before collecting samples.
- For product comparisons, use comparable pack, dosage, format, and price bases.
- For review analysis, disclose the sample source, size, date range, and sampling method.
- For a trend claim, seek more than one period or source and examine promotions or seasonality.
- For market share, require a defined denominator and methodology; otherwise describe observable channel presence only.

## Research Failure Modes

- confusing ingredient-market demand with downstream finished-product demand;
- counting duplicate SKUs or sellers as separate products or brands;
- treating e-commerce visibility as total-market coverage;
- assuming that a brand using the ingredient is automatically a reachable customer;
- confusing technical feasibility, regulatory permissibility, consumer appeal, and buyer willingness;
- reporting a precise number whose source only supports a range or directional comparison.
