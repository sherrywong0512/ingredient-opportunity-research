# Product Format Screening

Use this protocol after an application area passes the first property-to-need screen and before estimating demand, recommending experiments or choosing customers.

## 1. Define saleable formats

Translate a broad application into concrete products that have different formulation, process, exposure, pack or buying logic. Examples of format dimensions include:

- matrix and phase: water-based, oil-based, emulsion, powder, gel, solid or aerated;
- use mode: leave-on, rinse-off, ingestible, disposable, concentrated or diluted;
- process: heat, shear, fermentation, baking, sterilization, freeze-thaw or reconstitution;
- pack/dose: bulk, unit dose, sachet, pump, jar, spray, tablet or capsule;
- population/site: face, body, scalp, lip, infant, clinical-adjacent or general use.

Do not create formats solely because the ingredient could physically be added. Keep only formats with an observable product need and identifiable buyer market.

## 2. Screen every format

| Format | Product need | Ingredient job | Matrix/phase and process fit | Contact/consumption pattern | Evidence and use amount | Regulation/claim | Alternatives | Current SKU adoption | Format market evidence | Buyers | Result/gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Check:

- whether the ingredient reaches and retains its intended function in the actual matrix and use duration;
- solubility, dispersion, stability, sensory, packaging and interaction with other actives;
- exact finished-product use amount, unit basis and cost, including enabling ingredients or process costs;
- format-specific regulatory classification, exposure, warnings and claim evidence;
- current commercial examples with exact SKU/market/date labels;
- incumbent systems and switching barriers;
- manufacturers, brands and OEM/ODM buyers that actually produce the format.

Classify each format as `priority`, `conditional—technical test`, `conditional—market evidence`, `regulatory unresolved`, or `do not advance`.

## 3. Validate format-level market data

Do not use the parent application market as the format market. Record whether the number is:

- total retail sales, platform GMV, sell-in, shipment value, units, production volume, registrations/filings or launch count;
- full market, channel subset, platform subset, sampled panel or forecast;
- a standalone format or a bundle that contains multiple formats.

Use official statistics for the parent-market anchor when available, then use audited company/category disclosures, recognized retail panels, platform data, filings/registries or transparent databases for the format split. Preserve geography, channel, base year, metric, coverage and method.

- Platform GMV is not total retail market size and may include returns or overlapping definitions.
- Filing/launch count measures development activity, not sales or active SKUs.
- Bundles must not be allocated to component formats without a disclosed method.
- A format share from one channel cannot be applied to the total market without evidence of channel representativeness.
- When only `total × approximate share` is possible, show the formula and label the result a derived directional estimate or lower bound.

## 4. Connect format size to ingredient demand

Keep these separate:

1. **format commercial pool:** retail/GMV/units for the finished-product format;
2. **ingredient-addressable pool:** the share of format output where the ingredient can legally and technically create value;
3. **ingredient demand:** addressable output × likely adoption × use amount ÷ active concentration × loss factor;
4. **supplier-addressable demand:** ingredient demand further limited by supplier grade, authorization, capacity, geography, qualification and realistic win rate.

Do not convert retail value directly into ingredient tonnage. Obtain or estimate unit volume, average net content, adoption share and use amount, with separate low/base/high assumptions and sources. If any decision-critical input lacks an evidence basis, report the format pool but mark ingredient demand `not reliably estimable`.

## 5. Prioritize and design the next test

Prioritize formats only when product need, technical fit, regulatory route, market evidence and identifiable buyers align. A large format with weak ingredient need can rank below a smaller format with strong value in use.

For every priority format, specify the smallest format-specific validation:

- exact matrix and incumbent control;
- ingredient grade and amount ladder;
- process and packaging conditions;
- technical, sensory, safety/efficacy and shelf-life endpoints;
- cost and claim checks;
- success/failure rule and customer decision unlocked.
